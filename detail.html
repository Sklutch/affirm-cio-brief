1. Bottom line: the CIO take

Stance: cautious, because at $72.68 the stock already prices Affirm's bull case unless its beta normalizes, which it has not done in two years. The gap between price and value is the discount rate, not the operating forecast: the FY27 guide is above $64B GMV and 14.5% GAAP operating margin [S2 p11], and 30+ day delinquencies sit in a 1.97–2.88% band. Revisit for an upgrade if the rolling 1Y beta to the S&P 500 holds below 2.0 for a full quarter, and toward the bear case if FY27 GMV growth runs below the 25% Forum pace for two quarters.

3. Business model

Affirm underwrites each purchase, earns from both merchants and consumers, and sells most loans to capital partners. It keeps about 4.2% of every dollar of volume after transaction costs.

Merchant fees (MDR)
Paid per transaction; highest on 0% APR offers merchants subsidize. +30% YoY [S1]
Consumer interest
Simple interest, no late fees. 48.1% of revenue [S1]
Gain on sale / forward flow
Loans sold to partners; +56% YoY to $596.6M [S1]
Card interchange and servicing
Card network $294.0M; servicing $173.1M [S1]
RLTC (revenue less transaction costs)
$2,085M in FY26 = 4.15% of GMV. Revenue minus credit losses, funding, processing and 0% APR purchase costs: Affirm's gross profit on lending [S1][S2]

Funding
$30.0B capacity: forward flow 46%, warehouse 30%, ABS 24%. About 53% of the $20.2B portfolio is off balance sheet [S2][S109]

6. Expectations and what the price implies

Management guides above 27% growth for FY27 and targets $100B GMV by FY29. A reverse DCF shows that at today's beta the price needs about 24.5% growth a year from FY28 to FY36, close to the Forum's >25% pace held for nine years.

Beta, briefly: how much the stock moves per 1% market move. AFRM moves about 2.5–3× the S&P 500; the analysis uses β 2.7 (between 3Y daily 2.55 and 3Y weekly 2.83), giving a 16.3% cost of equity (4.99% risk-free + 2.7 × 4.20% equity risk premium [S35][S110]). Dynamic sensitivity: the same shocks with multiple re-rating (analyst calc)

Method: start from the market-implied path, the one reverse-DCF path that reproduces $72.68 at raw β 2.7 (24.5% growth a year FY28–36, 22.5% terminal GAAP margin). Translate each shock into operating-margin points on revenue (8.49% of GMV), then revalue the whole cash-flow stream. "One year" moves only FY27; "persistent" applies the shock to every year including the terminal value, which is how a re-rating shows up in a DCF. The funding shock is run through earnings (17.5bp of RLTC per 100bp) and through the discount rate (risk-free +100bp).

  • Partner deals are fading: 2021–24 wins averaged +19.2% abnormal day-0 (n = 6); 2025–26 wins +1.9% (n = 6); Welch t = 2.65, p = 0.036, significant at 5% but on small samples. Losses still hurt (Walmart −6.3%, n = 1).

The stock trades with UPST, SOFI and ARKK (correlations 0.72, 0.65 and 0.69, vs XLF 0.42 [S35]), which supports the high beta; in a factor regression the market and that peer group explain 57% of 1Y daily variance (analyst calc).
