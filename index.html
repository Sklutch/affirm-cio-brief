Affirm (AFRM): CIO executive brief

Cautious: at $72.68 the stock already prices Affirm's bull case. The business is on track; the price is the problem.

Analysis, not investment advice

1. Bottom line

Cautious: the business is executing, but at $72.68 the stock already prices in Affirm's best case.

  • At today's risk level the probability-weighted value is about $50.5, roughly 30% below the price (analyst calc).
  • Operations are not the issue: management guides FY27 volume (GMV) above $64B [S2 p11].
  • Upgrade if the stock's beta (see section 6) stays below 2.0 for a full quarter; turn more negative if GMV growth falls below 25% for two quarters.

2. History

From a 2012 start-up, through a 95% crash, to its first full year of GAAP profit.

  • 2012: Affirm founded [S1]
  • Jan 2021: Nasdaq IPO at $49 [S87]
  • Aug 2021: Amazon partnership; stock up 47% in a day [S59]
  • Nov 2021 → Dec 2022: Stock falls 94.7% from peak to trough [S35]
  • Mar 2025: Walmart's OnePay switches to Klarna (about 5% of volume) [S33]
  • Aug 2026: FY26: first full year of GAAP operating profit, $417M [S1]

3. Business model

Affirm lends at checkout, earns from both consumers and merchants, and keeps about 4.2 cents of every dollar it processes.

  • Consumer interest is the biggest line: 48% of FY26 revenue [S1].
  • Merchants pay a fee per sale, highest on 0% APR offers: 27% of revenue [S1].
  • Most loans are sold to funding partners; about 53% of the $20.2B loan book sits off balance sheet [S2][S109].

4. Financials

Fast growth, now profitable: FY26 volume rose 37% to $50.2B with the first full-year GAAP operating profit.

  • Revenue grew 32% to $4.26B [S1].
  • GAAP operating profit was $417M, a 9.8% margin [S1][S2].
  • Credit is steady: loans 30+ days late were 2.5% in the latest quarter, inside a 1.97–2.88% range since 2022 [S127].

5. New endeavors

Affirm Card is the only new engine already in the numbers; the rest are options for FY28 and beyond.

  • Affirm Card: $2.8B of volume in the June quarter, up 124% [S2 p04–05].
  • Affirm Bank: charter filed Jan 2026, still pending and opposed by bank groups [S34][S117].
  • Affirm Edge (pay over time on debit, with Fiserv): first pilots Jan–Jun 2027 [S2 p03][S42].

6. Expectations and what the price implies

Management and the Street expect strong growth; the price assumes Affirm keeps it up for about a decade.

  • Guide: FY27 GMV above $64B, about 27% growth [S2 p11].
  • Street: average price target $98.33 [S53].
  • What the price implies: about 24.5% growth a year from FY28 to FY36, close to management's own 25%-plus plan [S109] (analyst calc).

Beta, in plain words: how much a stock swings when the market moves 1%. Affirm moves about 2.5–3 times as much as the S&P 500, so investors demand a high return, about 16.3% a year [S35][S110], and that shrinks what future profits are worth today.

7. Biggest levers and what moves the stock

Investor risk appetite moves the value most, then funding costs, then how much Affirm keeps per dollar of volume.

  • Beta: a drop from 2.7 to 2.2 would lift value about 30% (analyst calc).
  • Funding cost: a lasting 1-point rise in rates cuts value about 20% (analyst calc).
  • Take rate: keeping 0.1 cent more per dollar of volume adds about 6% (analyst calc). On earnings days the stock moves 14.9% on average [S35].

8. Catalyst calendar

The next three weeks hold the key tests.

  • Nov 5, 2026: FQ1'27 results [S38]: watch GMV vs the $13.7–14.0B guide and late payments [S2 p11]
  • Nov 10, 2026: October CPI [S37]: rates hit the stock (a hot CPI day on Oct 14 cost 9.5% [S43])
  • Nov 15, 2026: $221.3M of convertible notes mature [S1]; net cash is about $1.5B (analyst calc)
  • Dec 8–9, 2026: Fed meeting [S36]: funding costs
  • Jan–Jun 2027: First Affirm Edge pilots [S2 p03]
